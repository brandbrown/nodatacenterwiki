## The issue

Data center cooling equipment and backup generators operate **around the clock**. Reported
interior noise levels can reach ~96 dB, and low-frequency hum travels for miles and
penetrates walls. Backup generators are tested regularly and run continuously during outages.

## The amphitheater effect

South Mountain and the Foothills form a natural bowl. Rocky desert terrain reflects sound,
and temperature inversions bend noise downward. Homes near the hills could experience noise
**roughly twice as loud** as they would on flat terrain. Nearby Chandler residents living
near an existing data center have filed years of noise complaints.

## The regulatory gap

No noise study demonstrating compliance with the **Phoenix noise ordinance** appears in the
public record for this approval. Residents commissioned an **independent acoustic study**;
the city's file did not contain one at the time of approval.

## Leverage

- Demand a **noise study on the record** demonstrating ordinance compliance before further
  approvals, accounting for terrain and inversions.
- Use the residents' **independent acoustic study** as a technical counterweight.
- Noise is enforceable once operational, but raising it now creates a record and can
  condition permits.
