## The issue

The campus is designed with **100+ diesel backup generators**. These are tested regularly
(monthly load-bank testing is typical per NFPA 110) and run continuously during any grid
outage. Diesel combustion emits **nitrogen oxides (NOx), fine particulate matter (PM2.5),
carbon monoxide, and hazardous air pollutants (HAPs)** - directly into a residential area
with no buffer zone. PM2.5 is linked to respiratory illness, heart disease, and premature death.

## Why this is the strongest opening

Large stationary sources of air pollution require **permits**. Depending on total potential
emissions, 100+ generators are likely to trigger review by the **Maricopa County Air Quality
Department** and/or **ADEQ**. Air permits typically include a **public comment period** - a
formal, legally recognized channel for residents to submit objections and technical concerns
that the agency must consider.

This is more concrete than the zoning argument, which is largely foreclosed. A permit that is
delayed, conditioned, or challenged on the record is a real obstacle to operation.

## What to watch for

- An **air quality permit application** naming the site or Menlo/Thistle entities.
- The **public comment window** opening (dates matter - they are short).
- The generators' rated capacity and **potential-to-emit** calculations.
- Whether the facility is classified as a **minor** or **major** source.

The watchdog scanner monitors Maricopa County and ADEQ public-notice pages for exactly these
events. When one appears, it will be flagged at the top of the [timeline](../../) and in the
internal digest.
